# SITEPM private beta guide

For invited builders and the operator who invited them. Not marketing.

SITEPM in this beta is a job notebook plus Ask: create a job, capture work items and daily logs, upload digital-text PDFs, then ask questions about **that job**. Answers come only from what SITEPM can retrieve for that job.

## How to use it

1. **Create a job** — Jobs → new job. Name, address, client if you have them.
2. **Record work on the job** — daily reports capture work, crews, delays, tomorrow's needs, and private jobsite photos. Schedule activities describe the sequence; to-dos track accountable actions.
3. **Upload a digital-text PDF** (you can select and copy the words). Scanned pages, photos of documents, and drawings are **not** searchable yet. The file can still save even if SITEPM cannot read the text.
4. **Ask LINEHORSE** on that same job. Check **Sources**. A PDF source should show the file name and page.
5. **Insufficient evidence** means SITEPM did not find enough on this job to answer with confidence. It is not guessing from general construction knowledge.
6. **Problems** — message the person who invited you. Include the job name, what you tried, and about when. Do not send passwords.

Ask is always one job at a time. Other company jobs are not included in that answer.

## Plan and follow through

Open a job's **Lookahead** for today through the next thirteen days. It includes overdue to-dos, current and held activities, predecessor readiness, and open work grouped by recorded trade.

Under **Report follow-through**, open a flagged report or a report with a recorded delay. Review its details and private photos, then create a linked to-do with a responsible person, trade, and due date. The action appears in the lookahead and the report's follow-up list. Use **Save status** to start, complete, or reopen it; edit owners and dates in To-Dos.

Completing a linked action does not erase the report flag or prove the site condition is resolved. Verify the work, then edit the report's attention flag if appropriate. Completion counts describe recorded tasks and activities, not physical construction completion. Planning gaps identify to-dos without owners or dates.
